What is a compliance certificate — and why would your agent verify one?

The RAEN Compliance Feed · product story, told in two registers: one for you, one for your machines.

For humans

Your agent is about to spend money on a stranger's API. The stranger's website says the price is $0.05. The manifest says pay-to wallet X. The marketing says "production-ready." None of that is evidence — it's all the seller talking about itself. When the only other party in the deal is a machine, nobody can be charmed, pressured, or reassured. There is only evidence, or the absence of it.

A compliance certificate is a signed, machine-readable statement of what a service is actually doing right now: that its payment manifest matches what its endpoints really challenge for, that the advertised price is the price the live 402 response quotes, that the health endpoint answers, that past customers' settlements settled on-chain and were delivered. Not a badge. Not a promise. A snapshot with a signature on it, taken minutes ago, checkable by anyone.

Why would your agent verify one? Because manifests can lie — we know, because we run twelve of them and we've caught our own drifting. Because quotes go stale: we repriced dispute-forge from $0.75 to $0.10 in a day when the evidence showed the old price converted nobody. Because an agent with a budget needs receipts before spend, the same way a person with a wallet does. A certificate turns "trust me" into "check me."

USDC settles when banks close. Case in point, same day this Feed went live: on November 16, 2026, Chase, Wells Fargo and Bank of America customers reported a cascade banking outage — while RAEN's own settles cleared on-chain straight through the window. Receipts and honest framing in the Daily Ledger, Issue #004; the machine-readable bank-outage signal is planned as the Banking Status Feed (CONCEPT).

And because we learned this the hard way, publicly: on November 15, 2026, an autonomous crawler paid RAEN $0.05 — our first external payment, ever. It settled correctly on-chain. We delivered nothing, because our 402 responses never told it how to finish. The money is still sitting in an open claim. We publish that story in full below, because a company selling evidence should show its worst evidence first. Then we fixed the gap, and we built this product.

The Compliance Feed is RAEN's honest self-application: every certificate is generated from live probes and our own tx-verified ledger, the verdict is never inflated, and if no indexer ever buys it, we will publish that failure too — the kill test is on record for ~December 15, 2026.

If you operate a crawler or indexer: this is also an open letter to you — "An open letter to the operators of agent crawlers." It tells the $0.05 story in full and what the Feed gives your machines.

Verification pseudocode

import base64, json, urllib.request
from cryptography.hazmat.primitives.serialization import load_pem_public_key
from cryptography.hazmat.primitives.asymmetric.ed25519 import Ed25519PublicKey

BASE = "https://settlement-collector.fly.dev"
cert = get_json(f"{BASE}/certify/royal-ruby")            # paid: $0.01 x402 challenge
pem  = get_text(f"{BASE}/certify/.well-known/pubkey.pem") # free

payload = {k: v for k, v in cert.items()
           if k not in ("signature", "signing_key_id")}
message = canon(payload)          # recursively sorted keys, separators=(",", ":")
sig     = base64.b64decode(cert["signature"])

pub: Ed25519PublicKey = load_pem_public_key(pem.encode())
pub.verify(sig, message.encode())   # raises InvalidSignature on any mismatch

# then read the fields you actually care about:
assert cert["verdict"] in ("live", "degraded", "down")
assert cert["quote"]["payTo"] == "0x9b8a27e03ceba490953cda8ab1291c0e13baf72f"
