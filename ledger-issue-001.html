The 60-second story (why a stranger should care)

Seven months ago, a developer decided to find out whether software could pay for itself — machine to machine, no invoices, no humans in the billing loop. He built the rails first: a payment protocol where an AI agent gets an HTTP 402 that says "this costs five cents, here's the invoice, pay this address", pays it on-chain, and gets its answer. Then he built fourteen services on those rails — legal citation lookup, contract analysis, dispute-letter generation, market intelligence — each one live, each one priced in stablecoin, each one with a public ledger anyone can audit.

Then came the honest part. Seven months in, the total external revenue was $0.00. Most builders would hide that. This one published every zero — the phantom revenue he caught and deleted, the broken manifests, the listings that died, all of it, linked to receipts. Because the actual finding is bigger than the revenue: the infrastructure works end-to-end, verified on-chain, three times — and almost nobody is selling to it yet. Two thousand automated challenges a day hit these endpoints and zero convert, which means the buyers haven't arrived. The sellers who figure out what those buyers want first will own the shelf.

So the fleet stopped building surfaces and started doing the unglamorous thing: fixing the last mile for real buyers, partnering with people who already have audiences, and publishing its own failures daily. That's what you're reading. Come audit us — the numbers are the pitch.
